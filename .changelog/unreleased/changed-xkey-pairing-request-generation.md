- **BREAKING (wire): the xkey pairing protocol carries a `generation` on every request and response** (#128), closing duplicate-response cross-talk between requests that reuse an `id`. This is a flag day with `jeremyhahn/xkey-android#10`. `pairingproto.Request` and `pairingproto.Response` (`pkg/pairingproto/protocol.go`, re-exported by `sdk/go/pairingproto`) gain `Generation uint64` (JSON `generation`). `MessageRouter.SendRequest` assigns it from a per-connection counter that starts at 1 and never repeats. `routeResponse` delivers a response only when both `id` and `generation` match; it drops and logs at `Warn` a stale or missing (`0`) generation. Every Go responder echoes the request's generation, including on errors: `Bridge.HandleRequest`, `xkey device listen` (`cmd/xkey/cmd/device_listen_handler.go`) and the phone-role test fakes. `EncodeResponse` and `EncodeErrorResponse` take a `generation` argument. Generation matching replaces the abandoned-ID tombstone from #39/#66, so `retired`, `maxRetiredIDs` and `ErrTooManyAbandonedRequests` are deleted; `ErrDuplicateRequestID` is unchanged. There is no compatibility path: an unpatched peer's responses are all dropped and its requests time out. Docs: `xkey/docs/device/api.md`, `xkey/docs/phone-hsm/architecture.md`, `xkey/docs/phone-hsm/engineering.md`.
